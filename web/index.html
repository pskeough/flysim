Memory runs on its own thread, five times a second. It updates only from what the fly perceives: things within sight range (a fire's glow carries furthest), outside a blind wedge behind it, not hidden by walls, plus what its feet taste and its antennae feel. Unseen things keep their last place, and confidence halves every 90 s. With memory on, the motor layer and the mind see only what is perceived now; the rest they get from memory.


          In contend mode memory bids for the motor neurons (weight × urgency × confidence) against the motor layer (0.35 while it holds an intent, 1.0 under your order). The higher bid drives. The mind's override beats both, and the fly's reflexes are never muted. In shadow mode it only watches and proposes. Aligned means memory and the gated system want the same direction. This is a demo mechanism, not a model of the fly's mushroom body.

How pain works: the pathway in the connectome
      
The pathway simulated

      

      
The entry is 20 ascending neurons chosen by their connectivity to the PPL1 punishment dopamine neurons: a STAND-IN for the ascending neurons that abdominal nociceptors reach in real flies (Jones et al. 2025), whose FlyWire identities are not known. Injury (heat above 38 °C in the body or on a surface it touches, the hand, slow tissue damage) drives them; the pain signal is what PPL1 does in response. Nociception for the fly; for the language model, a functional analogue. Nothing here shows that either one feels anything. Design: research/PAIN_DESIGN.md.
